# Assassin's Creed (2007)

**Score:** 5/10

-------

## Review

Assassin's Creed (2007) marks the start of a series that would eventually spawn fourteen mainline games, with nine of them following the same core formula established here. Attempting to evaluate this game without making comparisons to later entries in the series or other open-world action games such as *Ghost of Tsushima, Arkham City, Watch Dogs, etc*- is difficult. Those games have undoubtably conditioned me to appreciate QoL features that simply aren't present in Assassin's Creed. What's also difficult to ignore is the absolute **DISASTER** of a PC port this game is, as it's riddled with bugs that I'll humor in detail later. Nonetheless, I'll do my best to keep my thoughts as honest and fair as possible, setting aside both bugs and influence of later games.

To begin, I immediately want to get something off my chest: despite being the first in the series, Assassin's Creed to me, is really captivating in terms of presentation. The graphics while old because *cough* 2007, have held up better than I expected. It's quite nice to see that Altair's robes flow naturally with the wind and that characters have a lifelike feel to them thanks to motion-captured animations.

Another thing I want to immediately point out is that the place in which the game takes place in *really* cool. The Third Crusade and the Holy Land serves to be really fascinating and the cities the game allows you to explore only further piqued my interest. Not to mention, the cities themselves feel remarkably alive. You can overhear conversations, hear dogs barking in the distance and hear town criers spreading propaganda. Details such as these all add up to deliver an immersive atmosphere. 

Speaking of immersion, I like that the game reminds you that everything you are seeing is merely just a digital reconstruction of your ancestor's memories through the Animus. Data fragments ocassionally appearing over enemies, synchronization effects and collectibles, subtly reinforce that everything is a simulation. It's all implemented well enough that it's never distracting yet it's enough to notice if you're really paying attention. 

The tone of the game is also something that I very much enjoyed. It carries a almost horror-like atmosphere. In the beginning of the game, you awaken in the modern day, strapped to the Animus and watched over by two strangers in lab coats, all within a lifeless bare room. You are directed and essentially forced into the animus to relive your ancestor's memories in search of *something* of great importance. 

This setup creates a sense of urgency. You get the feeling that if you fail to find what you're looking for, or perhaps even when you do, something terrible will follow. This sense of urgency and dread lingers throughout the entire game and never lets go. I find this to be one of, if not the greatest strength this game has to offer. It's impressive because when other games attempt to do something similar, the tension will gradually fade away as the player becomes more comfortable or the story seems to forget the stakes. Assassin's Creed however maintains the uneasy atmopshere all the way to the end and by the end of the game, it leaves you with a feeling of uncertainty and curiosity for what's next in the series.

Now with that out of the way, it's time to discuss the not so pretty parts of Assassin's Creed:the gameplay. Now before I dive into my criticisms. I want to acknowledge something important. Assassin's Creed laid out a very strong foundation for the next nine games that followed its blueprint. The core gameplay loop of parkour, stealth and combat present in those games is made very well clear that Assassin's Creed 2007 succeeded in something that a lot of players including myself would continue to keep coming back for. 

Alright .. onto the criticisms and oh boy do I have criticisms.

First and foremost, the thing I **despise** the most about this game is the controls. What in god's green flat earth were they thinking with the controls? The amount of times I've fallen off a building or accidently missed the leap of faith and instead broke my back and desynchronized is just ridiculous. The game is very strict in how they want you to control Altair to the point that it's too restrictive and simply not fun.

Now, to be fair, I recognize that a large part of my frustration comes from me being accustomed to how later entries have you control your character. Admittingly, I'm also very stubborn to adapt this control scheme after spending so much time with the successors. That being said, it's still frustrating regardless and frequently got in the way of truly having fun with the game. 

This issue is only further exacerbated with how the game seems to encourage you to stick to rooftops in a very toxic way. It does this by filling the streets with dense  crowds that Altair struggles to quickly move through without tripping over. This is made even more frustrating by the presence of beggars and madmen. Beggars constantly stick to Altair like glue. Madmen are perhaps the biggest spawn from hell in Assassin's Creed as they will run up to you and shove you, which can chain into other madmen shoving you, killing all momentum.

I wouldn't mind the NPC's if the game provided meaningful solutions to deal with them. For example, if I could toss a few coins on the ground to distract the beggars or quickly shove madmen aside without going into full-combat mode; these solutions would have gone a long way in reducing frustration. Instead, the game leaves me with little practical options besides rooftops where I'm forced to deal with the clunky parkour controls.

Another major criticism I have with this game is that the mission structure becomes far too repetitive, even when considering that this was a 2007 release. The structure is essentially as follows: you travel to a city and speak with an Assassin at the bureau. They tell you something along the lines of *"Altair, you must collect information on [insert name] in order to kill him."*.

You collect information by completing missions that **always** follow this structure.

Eavesdrop a guy

Pickpocket a guy

Beat up a guy.

You return to the bureau after this is done and you get the go-ahead to kill the target. You repeat this process for about eight hours.

To the game's credit, this is actually enjoyable for the first few hours as it's satisfying to learn your targets whereabouts through these investigations. However once I realized that every single assassination in this game follows the exact same structure, I began counting how many assassinations remained until the end of the game.

#### Final Thoughts ####

Assassin's Creed (2007) is a game that hasn't aged all too from a gameplay perspective. The loop is repetitive and its controls are frustrating. However, the atmosphere and setting are exceptional. It established the foundation for the franchise and created a formula that many players would continue to enjoy in later entries.

Should you play the original Assassin's Creed? Probably not, it's old, frustrating and repetitive. It's possible that Ubisoft will remake Assassin's Creed for the 20th Anniversary. If that ends up being the case, probably play that instead!

Assassin's Creed is interesting to look back on and it kind of pains me to see what this game established was never *truly* fully realized. The foundation was so strong that I can't help but wonder what future entries could have looked like if the developers had chose to use this foundation as more of a basis of ideas rather than an exact copy-paste in core-gameplay formula for nine games. I suppose Ubisoft did eventually recognize this with the installment of the later RPG games such as *Origins, Odyssey, Valhalla and Shadows*. However those games warrant a different discussion on their own.
